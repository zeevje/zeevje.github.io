About

I'm a systems engineer working at the intersection of neurotech and embedded systems, with 4 years of experience on first-in-human BCI clinical trials at The Feinstein Institutes for Medical Research (Northwell Health). My day-to-day includes closed-loop stimulation control, real-time biosignal processing, and firmware development, all in the context of live sessions with tetraplegic participants. I'm co-author on two 2025 papers demonstrating restored cortically mediated movement and sensation in complete tetraplegia — one published in Nature Medicine, one currently under review. I'm interested in how that research translates into products that reach patients at scale.

Projects

Active Orthosis — BCI-Driven Grasp Restoration

The Feinstein Institutes for Medical Research

Designed and built a custom 3D-printed hand orthosis enabling a tetraplegic participant to grasp objects using decoded movement intentions from a BCI. Coordinated with a 3D printing lab to scan the participant's hand and produce a body with integrated slots for a servo motor and force sensor. Engineered a magnetic breakaway safety mechanism to protect the participant's fingers from excess tension. Developed ESP32 firmware to receive decoded motor intentions over SoftAP Wi-Fi, drive a servo via tendon-pull, and stream force sensor data back to the host over SPI at 50Hz. Later integrated a DQN reinforcement learning agent developed by a collaborator to dynamically halt or continue movement based on force feedback and user intent.

Neuromuscular & Spinal Cord Stimulation Firmware

The Feinstein Institutes for Medical Research

Extended the firmware of a Neuvotion neuromuscular stimulator to support wireless command reception, automatic pattern loading from decoded BCI output, and a ramping function that made stimulation feel more natural and comfortable. Built hardware synchronization across three devices — the Neuvotion stimulator, a custom ESP32-based spinal cord stimulator, and an intracortical microstimulator — using an oscilloscope to measure and align pulse timing. Added a blanking TTL pulse to suppress stimulation artifact in the decoder. Verified stable ISR operation at 2Hz, 50Hz, and 100Hz.

Object Discrimination Task — Sensory Restoration

The Feinstein Institutes for Medical Research

Developed embedded and host-side acquisition for a task testing whether a paralyzed participant could distinguish objects through cortical stimulation. Soldered tactile force sensors onto an ESP32, wrote firmware to stream sensor data over BLE at 50Hz, and implemented the MATLAB receiver on the host PC. When a force threshold was crossed, the system triggered cortical stimulation to restore a sense of touch — integrated with the existing BCI decoding pipeline running at 10Hz.

3D Finger Tracking System (GMHD)

The Feinstein Institutes for Medical Research

Built a markerless hand tracking pipeline for two clinical trials measuring finger aperture during therapy. Used two Orbbec Femto Bolt depth cameras with a MediaPipe hand landmark model to capture RGB-depth data and convert fingertip positions to 3D world coordinates at 20FPS. A master script ran two parallel processes — one prompting the participant through tasks, one handling camera capture and CSV logging. Later integrated a third-party GMH-D model that significantly improved accuracy and reduced frame drops. Trained clinicians on setup, operation, and troubleshooting.

Seizure Monitoring Wearable

Graduate Thesis · University at Buffalo · 2022

Developed a multimodal wearable seizure detection system integrating electrodermal activity, EMG, and accelerometry to classify grand mal seizure activity from regular movement. Upon detection, alerts and raw data were transmitted wirelessly to a receiver. A linear actuator on the receiver then extended to mimic an intramuscular midazolam injection — targeting seizure termination rather than alerting alone. The multimodal approach was chosen to improve sensitivity and accuracy over single-biomarker devices.
